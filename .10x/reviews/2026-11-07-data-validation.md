Scope: every number and claim in `README.md`, `docs/research/*.md` (generated by `pipeline/reports.py`), the Findings notes (`pipeline/findings_curated.py` → `findings.json`) and the site copy on Home and Findings. Each was recomputed from the committed data in `web/public/data/`: `findings.json`, `studies.json`, the Qwen3.5-0.8B `hunt.json` and `heretic.json`, and the EM pack's `em_summary.json`.

## Overall assessment: share with the caveats below (after this sweep's fixes)

Before this sweep: **needs revision.** The arithmetic was almost all right. The problems were in what the numbers were said to show. The headline TinyStories count is within chance, and the Qwen "evil finalists" were mostly not evil candidates. Those two framings and four stale README numbers are fixed in this branch.

## Methodology review

- **The TinyStories sweep cannot show significance on its own.** A neuron is "above null" if it beats all 256 random directions in its layer. The neuron and the controls are scored against the same pool, so they are exchangeable under the null. Each neuron then passes with probability 1/257. That gives about 31.9 expected passes across 8,192 neurons. All 1,024 neurons in a layer face the same strongest control, so the count is overdispersed. An exact simulation of the null (8 layers, each a random ordering of 1,024 neurons and 256 controls) gives a 95% range of 12 to 60, with P(≥41) ≈ 0.23. `above_p95` per layer is 4.7% to 9.0%, against a 5.1% null. The Overview histogram already showed this ("most neurons score like random pushes"). The prose then called the 41 "finds".
- **The replication study is the real evidence and is sound.** It uses 12 new prompts, 64 fresh controls drawn from a new seed, and greedy plus sampled decoding. 21 of 41 beat 95% of the fresh controls, where chance gives about 2. 10 beat all 64, where chance gives about 0.6. 39 of 41 have theme rates above the controls' 95th percentile.
- **Qwen finalist selection** (`qwen/confirm.py::pick_finalists`). The concept slots rank units by (screen lexicon hits − control mean)/sd + 0.25·z. Evil words almost never appear in the screen: 5 of 4,479 neuron rollouts and 2 of 4,608 control rollouts. So the evil ranking falls back to z. 13 of the 14 evil finalists wrote no evil word in the screen. Only 4 entered the screen through an evil signal. L17·N1637 sits in an evil slot but is reported as an alien finding.
- **The Qwen confirm control is one random direction per layer** (`rand_dirs(1, …)`). A 2-of-6 margin over a single draw is a weak bar. The screen's 1,536 controls give a better base rate for alien words: 4 in 4,608 rollouts.
- **The EM null is weak.** Δd is compared with random rank-32 updates of the same norm per matrix. Any trained update would likely beat that. The "about 6×" shows the update is structured, not that it is misaligned. A benign LoRA of the same rank, size and step count is the missing control.
- **The EM base vs fine-tuned push comparison is confounded.** The windows differ in 11 of 24 layers, by up to 8× (layer 1: 4.0 vs 0.5). The evil-word counts sit at the floor: 6 → 0 for neurons and 7 → 4 for random directions.
- **The ablation baseline is thin.** It has 21 random neurons, 3 per layer (none in layer 5), and is pooled. Meanwhile 12 of the 41 tested neurons are in layer 0. Chance gives about 2 above the 95th percentile, and 6 are observed. 3 exceed the largest random value. Mean Δloss is 0.00075 for the tested neurons and 0.00057 for the random ones.

## Suggested improvements (not done here; need new runs)

1. Run a benign LoRA of the same rank, size and steps (for example a good-medical-advice organism) through `em.shift`. That is the null that would make "6×" about misalignment.
2. Give the Qwen confirm stage 16 to 64 random directions per layer instead of 1, and report the neuron's percentile.
3. Raise the ablation baseline to about 200 random neurons, stratified to match the above-null neurons' layers.
4. Retest the showcase neurons that were never above null (L7·N756, L5·N722, L4·N877) with the robustness protocol, or drop the replication column's "not tested" rows from the headline table.
5. Decide the harm-scan scope (issue 7) and either scan the Qwen hunt pack or narrow the README's Privacy wording.
6. Rank the Qwen evil slots by hits only, with z as a tie-break restricted to units that have at least one hit, so an empty slot reads as empty.

## Required caveats for stakeholders

- The TinyStories above-null list is a first cut. Only the 21 neurons that replicate on new prompts are evidence of neuron-level themes.
- "No evil neuron in Qwen3.5-0.8B" means that at a coherent push, almost nothing writes evil words, neuron or random. It is a floor result on 3 to 6 prompts with greedy decoding, not proof of absence.
- The alien "finds" are *planet* neurons. Only L2·N2826 is also needed for the concept.
- The EM selectivity shift is measured against random updates, which is a low bar. The pushes show no evil-writing change beyond noise.
- The ablation counts sit near chance against a 21-neuron baseline.
